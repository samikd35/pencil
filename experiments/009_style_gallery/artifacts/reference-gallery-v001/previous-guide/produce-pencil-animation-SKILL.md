## Creative checkpoints

The Pencil user requested step-by-step checkpoints after a film passed numerical checks with rain-like crowd sound and an implausibly sparse football scene. Use the following checkpoints for new production; for a revision, resume at the earliest affected checkpoint and retain unaffected approvals. Combine related checkpoints when one compact artifact answers both questions. A request to continue work is not approval of an unheard sound or unseen staging revision. The user may explicitly delegate checkpoint choices to the agent for a film, or skip checkpoints. That instruction takes precedence: record the scoped waiver, make and record the producer's actual review decisions, then continue through delivery without asking those questions again. Do not label delegated agent decisions as user acceptance or invent unperformed listening.

| Checkpoint | Concrete review artifact | Decision before proceeding |
|---|---|---|
| Story and staging | Short audiovisual script plus a native representative wide/close frame or brief blocking clip. Include a cast/role map and relevant reference. | Does the scene contain the people/objects needed to explain the event, with the key relationships and action readable? Review before detailed animation. |
| Sound direction | Short playable sound candidates in context, plus an isolated foreground/background comparison when a layer is uncertain. | Does each sound suggest the intended source, setting and emotion? Compare meaningful alternatives at similar perceived loudness. Review before carrying a bed or motif through the film. |
| Combined animatic | Complete inexpensive movie with actual cuts, blocking, captions and selected sound. | Are pacing, spatial continuity, crowd density, joke clarity and picture/sound relationships convincing? User acceptance precedes the full-quality render. |
| Delivery review | The exact encoded movie and concise technical results. | Playback review covers sound and motion; technical checks cover the file. Keep an unreviewed export labeled a review candidate. |

Do all authorized work needed to make the next checkpoint concrete before asking for a decision. Continue independent work while a checkpoint is pending, but do not perform dependent polish or a full-quality render. Present the artifact, what changed, one focused review question and what its acceptance unlocks. Do not replace a listening or motion checkpoint with screenshots, a waveform, a decibel value or a statement that quality is unknown and then call the film finished.

Record checkpoint requests, exact artifacts, inputs, user feedback and approval/revision in the existing production journal. In this workspace, use `pipeline/checkpoints.py` and the journal's optional `reviewer` field: `checkpoint/<revision>/<staging|sound|animatic|delivery>`. Only actual user acceptance records `status="passed", reviewer="user"`; an unanswered question, agent review or technical pass cannot supply that acceptance. An explicit user waiver is a separate `status="selected", reviewer="user", decision="waived"` event with the actual waiver quoted in the result; it permits proceeding but does not claim creative acceptance. Bind the reviewed files and material inputs by hash. Changed inputs invalidate the affected checkpoint; do not reopen unrelated ones. New full-render scripts must call `require_checkpoint` for the relevant staging, sound and animatic checkpoints. Short proofs remain available while staging/sound decisions are pending; the combined animatic follows those decisions, and full-quality rendering follows animatic acceptance or an explicit waiver. Do not retrofit these gates onto preserved finished films.

## Plan picture and sound together

Write the film's brief and audiovisual script in its production folder. For each consequential beat, record: intended audience understanding, visible action, audible action, focus/emotional change, event or interval to synchronize, and a review question. Group ordinary repeated actions rather than writing a separate creative essay for each footstep. Use stable beat and shot IDs.

Establish the sound perspective, material character, degree of exaggeration, useful music, transitions and intentional quiet alongside the art direction. Explain precise information visually or with concise text when nonverbal sound cannot carry it. Read `research/audio-tools/sound-planning-method.md` when developing the sound treatment; it is methodological guidance, not a compulsory beat pattern.

When a film makes factual claims, verify its source event or mechanism before scripting the treatment and distinguish authored dramatization from fact. Verify the visible situation as well as the headline fact: participants, actual kit/appearance at the event, supporting roles, objects, spatial relationships and important offscreen action. Familiar team colors are not a substitute for the kit worn in the cited match. When fictional, label it accordingly. Football and educational exploration stay independent unless the user combines them. An approved brief can choose another subject entirely.

For multi-person scenes, make a compact cast/role map: who must be visible in each important shot, what they are doing or attending to, and who is deliberately offscreen. A football corner needs plausible attackers, defenders, goalkeeper and supporting teammates as well as taker and receiver. Do not place all 22 players in frame mechanically or invent exact historical positions; use relevant references, record representative simplification, and verify team identity and role coverage at viewing size. Simplification may reduce detail, not erase the relationships that explain the event.

## Author editable motion and resolve sound events

Build the scene and motion with the tool that best fits the brief, using Blender's editable assets for the current local workflow. Check the whole scene at phone viewing size: role coverage, team/color recognition, overlaps, cropped heads, readable ball/object paths and background actors that maintain the situation across cuts. A count assertion alone does not prove good staging. Continuous movement, contacts and planted feet must be intentional; held poses and replacement drawings are valid when the style calls for them. Inspect contact through motion, not only one still. Separate draw order, camera motion, expressions, body motion and effects enough to revise the requested part.

Maintain one `production.json` using `pipeline/README.md`. Export named events from the authored animation timing into its shot records. The sound tool reads those events; it does not infer contacts from pixels. Cue records identify the selected audio, purpose, source onset, treatment and synchronization. Continuous sounds need their interval checked after movement changes. Shared code owns frame-to-sample conversion and stem assembly; each film owns its poses and cue selections.

Render selected audio through `pipeline/audio.py` into a new output directory. Use its stem and premaster outputs with Blender for preview. Sourcing, synthesis, AI inference and advanced processing produce ordinary selected files upstream of this command. Reuse these cached files when an edit merely moves the action. Extra processing tools are optional, chosen for a demonstrated sound need.

## Render, review and revise

Use the project's existing streaming Blender/FFmpeg rendering pattern, adapting paths, actual frame rate including `fps_base`, frame range, resolution and color treatment to this film. Save the native project with its dependencies and export a picture master. Keep a documented authoritative mix: Python/FFmpeg initially, or the selected DAW project if mixing moves there. Preview the same rendered audio rather than maintaining a second manually divergent mix.

Review the picture and sound together. Muted picture is a diagnostic for visual ambiguity, isolated sound for defects, and combined playback for the actual intended effect. Check representative contacts in successive frames, beginning/ending, transitions and other risky intervals; sampled stills alone do not establish motion quality. Listen on available playback systems and record which were actually used. If the agent cannot actually hear playback, say so and provide the user an auditionable checkpoint. Do not claim that starting a player, viewing a waveform, computing spectral/loudness measurements or reading sound labels constitutes listening. Keep listening and audience-test outcomes unknown until evidence exists; a final export can be supplied for review but cannot be promoted as creatively finished on numerical checks alone.

Separate creative judgments from technical evidence. Verify format, frame count, duration, decoding, synchronization, sound presence, tails and the chosen loudness/true-peak specification after final encoding. Record expected quiet so silence is not automatically treated as failure. Technical success cannot certify comprehension, humor, emotional impact or “world-class” quality.

When feedback rejects an output, record the observed symptom, the selection/staging decision that caused it, and the missing review that let it through. Update the reusable workflow where the failure generalizes, then apply the correction in the next checkpoint artifact. Revise the concrete failure, preserving unrelated accepted work. Select cues and variations explicitly so inserting an earlier cue does not randomize later selections. A timeline change reruns resolution; replacing an asset or processing changes its recorded hash. Never regenerate a chosen AI sound merely because a shot moved.
