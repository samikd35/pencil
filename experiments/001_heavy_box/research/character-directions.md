# Character directions — Heavy Box

Researched 9 September 2026. **Selection pending.** These are original proposed designs informed by references, not copies or existing assets cleared for reuse. The comparison board is concept art, not a rig or proof of animation quality.

## Four choices

| Choice | Proposed appearance | Our animation judgment |
|---|---|---|
| A — Pure action | Faceless circular head, single-line torso, uniform limbs, tiny rounded hands/feet | Strong test of body acting. The final suspicious reaction must read through posture and head tilt. |
| B — Expressive doodle | Large outlined head, dot eyes, eyebrows, small mouth, single-line body, readable hands and feet | Recommended starting direction: preserves stickman simplicity while supporting effort and comedy through face and body. |
| C — Elastic comic | Rounded head, small bean torso, sweeping flexible limbs, mitten hands, oval shoes | Broad comic acting and recoil; controlled stretch and clear contacts will need testing. |
| D — Geometric mascot | Smaller circular head, compact rounded rectangular torso, stick limbs, minimal face | Distinct silhouette and a stronger sense of mass; farther from a pure stick figure. |

All choices are presented in comparable neutral and pushing poses on the same plain background. These choices do not dictate Blender techniques. After selection, test the appropriate combination of rigging, curves, and authored alternate drawings.

## Sources and what they support

1. [Pinterest: Ian Smith's Stick figure poses board](https://uk.pinterest.com/ianthedog/stick-figure-poses/). Successfully opened board text, pin descriptions, and links. It collects gesture drawings, expressions, sketchnotes, and little-people references, including links identifying Eva-Lotta Lamm. Discovery source for A/B; it is a mixed collection, not a coherent character model sheet or primary authorship record. Individual images were not all inspected.
2. [Pinterest: expressive stick figure pin](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/646688827773650968/). Research agent opened the pin; metadata describes a smiling figure with outstretched arms and attributes it to Shutterstock. Root's later fetch failed. Original individual artist and reuse terms were not established. Discovery reference for B only.
3. [Alison Beere: illustrated stickman construction guide](https://alisonbeere.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/how-to-draw-a-stick-figure-step-by-step-pdf.pdf). Creator's five-page guide. Extracted text specifies a circular head, spine, shoulders/hips, segmented limbs, oval hands/feet, joints, and proportion/pose variations. Supports the simple construction vocabulary of A/B; does not prove automated animation. The document is copyrighted reference material.
4. [Battle Axe: Rubberhose styles](https://battleaxe.co/rubberhose/docs/styles). Official documentation separates a limb's visual appearance from how it bends, including bend count and roundness. Supports exploration of C. This is an After Effects tool reference, not an adopted dependency or purchase recommendation.
5. [Doug Neill / Verbal to Visual: Drawing Stick Figures Playing Baseball](https://verbaltovisual.com/drawing-stick-figures-playing-baseball/). Creator article describing action using brick people and crediting Scriberia for that format. Reference for D's compact torso and gesture. Embedded video was not fully reviewed.
6. [Bloop Animation: Alan Becker's stick figure course](https://www.bloopanimation.com/stick-figure-animation-course-by-alan-becker/). Creator/course-provider reference for a motion-led stick-figure workflow encompassing planning through export. Agent opened the page; a subsequent fetch timed out. Supports A as a direction, not a prediction of our animation quality or a recommendation to buy a course.
7. [Pictoplasma: Walk With Character](https://home.pictoplasma.com/news/past-call-for-entries-walkwithcharacter). Organizer's showcase/call emphasizes character-specific stride, timing, and footwork. Useful motion-reference collection across designs. Historical contest requirements do not become this project's rules.
8. [Pictoplasma / BARBAR Studio: basic walk template](https://pictoplasma.de/download/pdf/WalkWithCharacter_Template1.pdf). Linked and credited by the organizer. Agent opened the PDF; visual screenshot retrieval timed out. Retain as a future reference, not a claim that its individual poses were inspected or reproduced.

## Conclusions for this experiment

- Research supports four different design vocabularies; the recommendation of B is our creative judgment, not a claim made by these sources.
- Separate character appearance from motion method. An expressive simple figure need not inherit the starter's detailed fighter masks or cutout limits.
- Compare the same action at the same scale. Different backgrounds, effects, or rendering polish would distract from character selection.
- No source artwork was imported as a production asset. The generated comparison uses written descriptions of broad design traits, with no reference image passed to the generator.
- Model capability, clean deformation, and production quality remain untested until motion is authored and reviewed.

## Access limits

Pinterest access varied: one board opened, another returned 403; an individual pin opened for the research agent but failed on a later root fetch. Image search was rate-limited. No complete visual review of all Pinterest pins, course lessons, or embedded videos is claimed. Research findings are a focused shortlist, not an exhaustive survey.

## User decision

Choose A, B, C, or D; combinations are welcome. B is recommended for the heavy-box comedy test, while A gives the strictest body-acting test. No preference is recorded as accepted until the user chooses.
