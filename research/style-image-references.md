## How the references govern production

The existing selector copies the chosen image as `sample.png` and its source, credit and quality targets in `option.json`, alongside the recipe and construction guide. These files are hashed and version-pinned inside the film. A catalogue update never upgrades an existing choice silently.

The production skill reads the pinned image and targets, designs the story’s own native artwork, and compares a representative finished frame beside the reference at the existing staging checkpoint. Reviews cover silhouette/proportions, expressions, head/body coherence, visible hands and continuous joins, line/edge quality, palette/texture and whole-scene composition. The animatic and delivery then check that the design survives the action. Visible quality gaps require artwork/staging revision, not a silent downgrade to the earlier box-test appearance.

## Source and image handling

- Images come from creator portfolios, production studios, official brands/channels, Disney D23 and the film distributor Gebeka. Four selections use actual illustration from official/creator YouTube upload images: stickman, whiteboard, pixelated and motion-comic. They are static artwork references; no video footage was downloaded.
- Original source files, acquisition notes and hashes are kept in the versioned reference-gallery artifact. Non-PNG files are encoded as PNG without cropping, resizing, sharpening or changing decoded pixels. Transparency and publisher marks are preserved. The menu uses contain-fit placement.
- Caricature is published at 800×600 and silhouette at 732×691. Both were inspected at full size and menu size; no HD or upscaling claim is made. The remaining images are at least 1180 pixels wide.
- The midcentury image is Disney’s *Toot, Whistle, Plunk and Boom*, which D23 explicitly describes as using UPA-style limited animation. It is a Disney production.
- The parallax card uses Elaine Lee’s *Yuki 7* Hong Kong style frame, credited by Chromosphere. It illustrates layered staging; the source production’s extensive 2D/3D pipeline and free camera movement are not claims about the current Pencil actor. The motion-comic image is cinematic illustration, not evidence of animated panel timing from a still.
- These are attributed reference images, not licensed production characters/backgrounds. The film’s own artwork is authored to the selected quality target.

## Visual review corrections

A crowded stickman poster was replaced with a clear action scene; the midcentury choice was changed to an unmistakable classic animation frame. A rounded-character candidate with distracting injury imagery was replaced with a clean Moth Studio/Headspace scene. The first food-character WEBP looked different when its alpha was discarded; the final choice is an opaque author-published Suki-Ya JPEG whose decoded pixels are preserved. Rejected candidates remain in the revision record.
