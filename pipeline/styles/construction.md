# Connected artwork over simple controls

Read this with the selected option's recipe. Existing routes include native curves/meshes, fitted illustrated surfaces and matched drawings. Choose the route for the actual performance, view, occlusion and finish. A new Grease Pencil or other construction route needs its own scoped runtime and visual proof; a style label does not switch the backend.

## The selected image sets the visual target

The current menu uses attributed artwork from external creators and studios. It is the standard to aim for in design and finish, not a render made by this pipeline. Earlier native guide images remain internal construction evidence. Reuse simple control mechanisms where they fit the new construction; the old benchmark character is not the finished art direction for a new story.

Build for the actual shot. Assess reusable character artwork and rig controls separately from the animation performed with them:

- **Same character, new action:** retain suitable artwork and controls; author the new performance. Different movements do not automatically require new construction.
- **Different character:** develop its intended design. Reuse compatible components, control mechanisms and construction knowledge, adapting proportions and attachments where needed; a shared style alone does not establish compatibility.
- **New camera angle or extreme pose:** first check what the existing asset supports. Add missing views, expression/hand drawings or pose corrections, or change the construction where necessary. A new angle does not automatically require rebuilding the whole character.
- **Reusing animation:** assess the performance separately, including control compatibility, proportions, timing, weight, contact and expression. A similar action is a candidate, not proof of suitability; adapt it to the shot or animate afresh when it does not fit.

Check uncertain adaptations in a focused pose/motion proof from the intended camera, using the existing creative checkpoints. Record the reuse or adaptation and its tested limits in the existing brief or journal. Never weaken the intended character, action, camera or finish simply to fit an old asset. Keep compatible parts and create only what the shot needs; the Heavy Box benchmark's limits do not define what future films may attempt.

This distinction follows Toon Boom's separation of [character master templates and action templates](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-20/premium/library/about-template.html), with [replacement drawings added during animation](https://helpcentre.toonboom.com/hc/en-ca/articles/41014001536275-How-do-I-add-a-substitution-drawing-while-animating-a-rig). These are workflow references, not evidence that our current builder supports every action or view.

## Asset requirements from performance

Block the intended interaction/action and coverage before finalizing detailed assets. Record required views, expressions, hand/prop relationships, support and transitions in the existing shot plan. Reuse identity artwork separately from animation. A standing neutral drawing is a construction source, not a performance to stretch across the film.

Give each image request a purpose: **identity/design** (coherent whole subject and finish), **construction** (source view, separable parts, hidden surfaces and registration), or **performance** (the needed expression, silhouette, contact or transition). One image may serve several purposes when it actually meets them. Derive prompts from the shot's intent and selected design, not dialogue alone. Identify reference-only artwork versus artwork intended to appear in the render.

Inspect generated results before rigging: identity, proportions, pose meaning, hand orientation/grip, ownership of overlapping limbs, opposite stride phases, cropped/missing parts, markings and continuity with adjacent drawings. Prompt wording is not proof of compliance. Correct a failed component or use suitable native construction; repeated generation of a larger all-purpose sheet is not the default repair.

Use deformation where the actual surface supports it. Supply coherent alternate views, facial/hand drawings, breakdowns or full poses where it does not; ordinary acting can require new drawings too. Preserve line, palette, proportion and head-to-toe finish across representations. A fixed face cannot become a profile through in-plane rotation, and a painted open palm cannot become a grip through wrist rotation alone. Intentional held drawings remain valid when their transitions and meaning serve the scene.

## Evaluate the current production

Evaluate this film against its brief and chosen references. After style `resolve`, read the pinned `sample.png`, `option.json` reference/credit/quality targets and recipe. The references establish craft and visual language, not reusable movie artwork. Compare relevant excerpts at comparable viewing size; stills alone cannot establish motion or sound quality.

Create a compact **unified visual treatment** inside the staging material: subjects and environment together, line/shapes, palette/value hierarchy, shading/light intent, scale/depth and camera language. Use rough layouts to test focus, negative space, overlaps, required views and screen direction. Choose drawn shading, flat color, masks or actual lights to suit the medium; lights cannot relight detail baked into an unlit image. Check foreground/middle/background, grounding and adjacent-shot color/shadow continuity. A held camera may be stronger than added drift or parallax.

Use the following whole-result review within the existing checkpoints, recording the actual artifact/interval, finding and remaining gap for applicable rows. It is not an automatic quality score or a requirement that every shot use every technique.

| Quality | Visible evidence |
|---|---|
| Art direction | Subjects, setting, lines, shapes, palette and shading belong together. |
| Character/subject design | Readable silhouette and identity; proportions, clothes and finish survive the required poses. |
| Expressive movement | Motivation, weight, anticipation, contact and recovery fit the chosen style. |
| Timing | Holds, accents, overlaps, reactions and cuts fit the moment and selected sound. |
| Poses and expressions | The intended thought/state reads; body, gaze and face agree through transitions. |
| Composition | Clear focus and purposeful framing/cuts; readable overlaps and spatial relationships. |
| Color and lighting | Controlled value hierarchy, intentional shading/light and consistency across adjacent shots. |
| Environment and depth | Perspective, scale, occlusion, grounding and relevant layers work in the actual camera. |
| Clean animation | No unintended contour changes, disconnected parts, pops, sliding, jitter or mask/shadow errors. |
| Visual storytelling | The consequential cause and response are visible; speech does not conceal missing action. |
| Secondary details | Relevant listeners, eyes, clothing, props and effects support focus rather than moving mechanically. |
| Distinctive style | The selected production's identity is coherent; references inform craft rather than replace it. |

For non-character work, judge the corresponding subject/state relationships; anatomy, likeness and facial acting are conditional. Inspect normal-speed playback for intention/timing, successive frames for transitions/contact, native-size crops for surface defects and the complete cut for continuity. An independent reviewer can challenge an assessment, but must state exactly what they viewed/heard. A launched player, waveform or contact sheet is not a playback/listening review.

For a repair, state what failed and what must remain good. Compare equivalent framing/speed through entry, peak, recovery and affected adjacent cuts. Check appearance, anatomy/surfaces, acting, timing, contact and sound relationship together. Save/reopen and run the intended next operation using the [source-ownership convention](../README.md#current-selections-and-correction-ownership). A new source must not silently replace the corrected file. Technical passes do not clear an appearance or performance regression.

**Start with the weakest frame.** Rank visible defects and changed regions before capturing review images. Inspect a native-size crop and an enlarged crop of the worst joint, contact or overlap, then the preceding/next frames and the entry, peak and recovery of that action. Compare the same region and framing before/after; preserve aspect ratio. After a local fix, make a brief complete-frame check for new cropping, layer or adjacent-part errors. Revisit stable regions only when the change can affect them or the wider check finds a regression. Record frame numbers, crop bounds, observed faults and actual coverage. This focuses review effort; it does not replace playback or promise that future drawings need no review.

Bind local selected context, construction sources and `assets/style-selection.json` when present. Recheck design through the animatic and encoded delivery; use actual observed evidence and leave unreviewed qualities unknown. These are the existing staging, sound, animatic and delivery decisions, with no separate style database or approval process.

## Demanding motion before detailed animation

Select the smallest representative sequence **from the actual story** that exposes unresolved performance, construction and look risks. Cover ordinary acting/interaction and demanding mechanics where both occur; also include enough camera/edit and environment coverage to judge continuity. Finish enough native coverage to test the artwork in action and use rough surrounding shots for context. No old player, fixed duration or unrelated test film is required.

Author it with [motion planning](../motion-planning.md): intention/reference, complete key poses, contact and breakdowns, then timing, secondary action and cleanup. A convincing jump cannot certify listening or facial expression; a blink cannot certify a run. For intense action, show preparation, contact/release, travel and recovery with readable support, weight and silhouettes. Quiet films and mechanisms use their own consequential changes.

Inspect actual evaluated surfaces and intermediate poses, not just endpoints or control counts. Keep contacts visible; camera moves must not hide construction failures. Add missing views, correctives or controls before propagation, while preserving accepted whole-subject design. Check energy at normal speed, then contact/bend intervals frame by frame. Save/reopen native edits and verify the selected operation preserves corrections. Record the reviewed scope and unknowns.

Reuse earlier evidence only within its demonstrated construction/action/view limits. New actors, views, poses and relationships need relevant coverage. Short finished proofs remain possible at staging; full-film quality rendering follows animatic acceptance or scoped waiver. No additional approval gate is introduced.

## Character facing, faces and anatomy

Apply these checks when using illustrated human-like characters; they are not prerequisites for diagrams, non-character motion graphics or unrelated construction techniques.

Specify body facing, head facing and gaze target separately for consequential acting. A fixed three-quarter drawing cannot supply other views merely by moving the camera or pupils. Prepare matching views and intermediate turn poses when required; inspect the turn and its neck/shoulder continuity in motion. Do not automatically face every actor toward the camera or use an edge-on flat drawing as a profile view.

Fit eye shape, count, tilt and gaze to each drawn face view. Classify the actual drawing as frontal, three-quarter or profile before fitting facial features: a three-quarter view may need a smaller visible far eye, while a true profile may hide it. Inspect enlarged face crops and actual output-size frames for eye–nose, eye–outline and brow–eye clearance during blinks, gaze shifts and head motion. Contact-sheet thumbnails alone cannot pass facial construction.

Check both the rest drawing and animated silhouette for extra bends: proportion adjustments intended for the head must not warp raised arms across a horizontal cutoff. Trace shoulder → elbow → wrist → fingers and pelvis → thigh → knee → boot through changed bend/contact/turn intervals. Normalized weights or connected control endpoints do not prove correct anatomy. Check residual source limbs, contour continuity, skin/garment agreement, planted contacts and prop overlap. Carry user-identified defects into subsequent cast and shot reviews; record actual coverage rather than claiming every pixel was cleared.

Review support and balance before interpolating a whole-body action. Fixed bone lengths and planted shoes can still produce an implausible squat when hips and torso are moved independently. Author the supporting foot or seat, weight transfer, hip path, knee direction, ankle orientation and recovery together; do not use one screen-space bend direction as a gait. For a carried prop, review the arm, wrist orientation, grip, clearance and receiving surface as one action. Samtoons 003's S07 v003 was rejected for these failures despite passing length/contact checks; its journal records the missed review. S09's small seated cup action does not validate standing or extreme motion.

Evidence: the Samtoons [audit](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/001-manchester-derby-2026-09-13/research/animatic-audit-v001/audit-and-repair-plan.md) and subsequent face/arm feedback in that video's journal. These checks apply across niches using this technique; successful repair of one shot does not validate all poses.

For reference-derived articulated drawings, assign **anatomical surface ownership before weights**. Coincident screen positions can belong to a prop, grip, forearm and torso at different depths. Shared fill/shadow/outline deformation applies within the same continuous surface, not indiscriminately across those overlaps. Complete hidden garment and sleeve artwork; keep rigid props registered to the grip. A traced color region is not automatically a body part.

Test evaluated contours, not just joint endpoints: a smoothed concave sleeve can self-intersect even with fixed bone lengths. Use correctly layered complete segments or authored corrective drawings where the intended bend needs them. At a moving wrist, register the hand and cuff boundaries; closed cut edges that rotate independently can expose gaps or double outlines. Remove residual source prop/paper/cuff fragments from isolated hands. Keep flat ink geometry planar: a converted tubular stroke can protrude through a foreground hand despite its centre having the correct depth.

These are scoped findings from the [Video 003 call reconstruction](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/003-barca-emergency-meeting/artifacts/staging-v001/call-performance-v004/README.md). Its source coordinates, masks, wrist range and fixed view are shot-specific. New views, grips and extreme bends may require new drawings; this is not a universal rig or a benchmark-parity claim.

## Construction rules from the box and trio tests

The following mechanisms describe the tested curve/mesh construction route. Use them when adapting that route; they do not require every new medium or character to use these same objects or limb curves.

- Keep motion controls separate from artwork. Preserve hand/foot contact targets; derive shoulders, hips and bend controls when proportions change. A good drawing alone does not establish a usable character.
- Use one live continuous curve for each limb. The hand and foot attach to the same endpoints that drive that curve. Put cuffs and sleeves on exact driven subcurves (`curve_section`), not independent images or separately keyed pieces.
- Combine shirt, hips and shorts fills before outlining their external silhouette. The existing `Unified body` compositor pass avoids independently outlined waist caps. Different shirt/shorts colors can meet inside that silhouette; never expose an unfilled joint gap. Check sleeve/body ordering separately: a closed chest outline drawn over moving sleeves can create an unintended separate vest. Join the garment silhouette at the shoulders and inspect lowered, raised and bent-arm poses; shared joint coordinates alone cannot resolve this.
- For deforming flat mesh garments, use explicit connected triangles/quads through bends, including shading and outline strips. A large concave filled polygon can produce triangles crossing outside the contour as shape keys deform it. Inspect successive frames and expose the full body when furniture hides its joins. Samtoons video's 002 [v008 construction notes](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/002-how-arsenal-won-the-league/artifacts/animation-v008/README.md) record the local repair; its character design remains pending user review.
- Draw head, hair, facial features and clothing in a coordinated visual language. Use native art attached to one head control, with a continuous neck behind the collar. Do not paste a photographic head onto a flat cartoon body. Likeness needs deliberate character design and review.
- Preserve character-specific identity when reusing controls. Derive head silhouette, feature spacing, hair, age/build, garments and footwear from that character’s selected design; a shared generic face with new colors cannot inherit a previous character’s likeness approval. Compare full-body and face views with the accepted finish, then inspect them through the shot’s motion. This belongs to the existing visual/staging review.
- Derive deforming sleeve shadows and decoration from the same fitted boundary as the garment. A shadow built from the original joint centerline can protrude after the shoulder boundary is fitted to the torso; topology connectivity alone does not prevent this. Check the actual visible surface at arm extremes.
- Build neck skin between jaw/skull and collar anchors in a consistent coordinate space. When mirroring a head, preserve the left/right ordering of those attachments; crossed endpoints create a folded or detached-looking neck. Keep a crew-neck opening open: a filled collar patch must not cap the neck with garment color.
- Treat shorts and other garments spanning multiple limbs as one designed garment. Preserve a shared waist, crotch and both hems with coherent folds/markings; independently decorated hip and thigh caps can read as multiple garments even when the joints touch. Fit the selected drawing to those boundaries and inspect standing, spread and passing poses.
- For illustrated facial replacements, inspect where the drawing ends. Do not stack a closed, wide neck stump onto a second narrow neck. Join a jaw/skull attachment that follows the head to a collar attachment that follows the torso; check the resulting neck length, width, side contours and front-collar overlap in motion. Register replacement drawings against stable skull/eye landmarks rather than a mouth-dependent bounding box, and add intermediate expressions when a direct swap visibly pops. A 24-fps export or coincident endpoints alone does not establish natural anatomy or smooth acting.
- Derive short-body proportions from the pelvis, with a floor/contact clearance suitable for that character. The benchmark's `max(body_y*0.70, max(foot_y)+0.78)` is an example, not an anatomical rule. Long reaches and low crouches may need new authored poses.
- Keep floor contact geometry unchanged when testing appearance alone. When feet change shape, recalculate their floor offset and recheck the full planted interval; endpoint agreement alone cannot prove no sliding.
- Treat draw order as authored shot design. Both arms in the stage pass get hidden when the body is composited above them; changing object Z cannot fix that. `pipeline/character_layers.py` separates the near arm, its driven sleeve and hand into foreground passes. The near arm/hand are in front by default; the scene's `front_arm_over_face` control can put the arm behind the face for an authored pose. Decide prop occlusion for each shot. Full turns, foreshortening, precise fingers, cloth simulation and quadruped rigs are outside the current proof.
- Match derived shoulders to the intended arms. In the box benchmark, the near hand rests screen-left: its shoulder must also be screen-left. The old opposite-side anchors made the arms cross the torso. Correcting these anchors changes upper-arm geometry while preserving the original hand, elbow and timing controls. Inspect the wrist, sleeve and shoulder together at rest, reach and recovery; do not move the hand target merely to hide a layering fault.
- Keep texture low contrast at moving seams. The sample's procedural Generated coordinates are deterministic and object-bound, but curved limbs can stretch patterns as their bounds change. Specific garment prints belong on stable body-local artwork; demanding texture tracking needs authored UVs or an additional proof.

## Cast-expansion failure: visible surfaces and timing

The [Samtoons full-animatic audit](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/001-manchester-derby-2026-09-13/research/animatic-audit-v001/audit-and-repair-plan.md) found continuous earlier actors beside new actors with roughly cut raster arms, generic replacement curves, residual artwork and visible joint covers. Numerical wrist attachment checks passed despite these defects. This establishes a failed adaptation, not a tested replacement recipe.

For a new drawing, fit its actual silhouette, garment boundaries and joint overlap; do not copy pixel thresholds or approximate weights as if they described every body. If replacing a limb, inspect the complete visible shoulder-to-hand and pelvis-to-boot contours in all required poses, including residual source art, material/outline agreement and depth order. Character appearance belongs to asset identity, not a scene object's name. Preserve the selected simple controls where suitable; add fitted surfaces, corrective drawings or missing views where the shot needs them.

Name the actual construction accurately: raster artwork on a deformable mesh, traced vector geometry, and native Grease Pencil strokes are different assets. None alone establishes better animation. Samtoons002’s first-batch v012 was **rejected**, despite technical contact checks: refitting new art to old controls changed proportions and motion together, and sleeves distorted. Its v013/v014 replacements also failed likeness or head/body coherence. Preserve these as failed adaptations, not reusable approved recipes.

For a reconstruction, compare the actual complete source drawing, source-fitted rest geometry and accepted action independently. Do not transfer another drawing’s garment thresholds, bone lengths, neck/sole registration or resting offsets unchanged. In the [bounded Gyökeres proof](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/002-how-arsenal-won-the-league/artifacts/gyokeres-proof-v015/README.md), regular source grids and continuous weights removed detected foldovers; new resting registrations removed inherited support shear. That candidate remains subject to combined visual/motion review. Test recognition, whole-body appearance, joints/clothes, contacts, facial acting and normal-speed energy together. A repair cannot be adopted by improving one of these while regressing another.

Check a kick as a coordinated support-foot, pelvis, knee, ankle, boot and ball action. A point matching at one frame does not establish approach, surface contact, release or recovery. Inspect the full changed contact/bend/turn intervals frame by frame and at normal speed; record actual coverage. Retain purposeful holds, but distinguish them from sparse poses stretched to fill a duration. Do not adopt a new construction across the cast until its visible result meets the current film’s accepted standard.

## Optional Heavy Box regression check

Use this only when reproducing the earlier test or checking a change to the mechanisms exercised by the Heavy Box scene. It is not required for selecting a style, building a new character or accepting a production. The general evaluation method is the current-film review above; a box test covers only its own construction and action.

The archived native guide images use an open-arm still pose so both hands are visible, including on wide mascots. `render_sample` saves that separate `sample-pose.blend` and reopens it to check wrists and hand framing. The `character.blend` saved before the still pose retains its motion. These internal tests are separate from the external images now shown in the menu. A static presentation pose is not an additional animation proof.

1. Save/reopen native artwork and edit a hand target: limb and hand must remain joined.
   Evaluate geometry in the non-rendered `Character editing` view layer. The stage pass now excludes the foreground arm, so its dependency graph cannot verify that arm's evaluated shape.
2. Compare all source control poses and contact geometry when the task only changes appearance.
3. Inspect successive frames of reach, maximum push, release, stumble and recovery. Check actual visible hands, wrists, collar, waist, feet and crossings at phone size. A numerical endpoint check does not prove layering.
4. Keep rerun outputs in a new versioned experiment directory and compare the affected behavior with the previous result. Preserve the original scene, artwork, motion, sound and evidence.
5. Keep evidence scoped. A benchmark's still/connection status describes only our internal native test. `Motion proof` needs inspected sequential frames/clip too. Neither establishes reference-level artwork, audience appeal, likeness, every angle or all possible actions.

## Recurring environments and camera continuity

Build a recurring location from one shared native set or linked collection. Give furniture, entrances, screens and other story-relevant props stable physical positions; use cameras, lighting and explicitly animated actions to change the shot. A concealed person and their identity reveal occupy the same set. A reverse angle may change screen positions but must follow the same geography, not a separately invented room. Check adjacent cuts for scale, pose, prop state and screen direction. Preserve intentionally different locations.


### Scoped hand replacements: preserve unrelated anatomy

A gesture-specific hand must solve the whole visible chain: sleeve, elbow, forearm, wrist, fingers and contact. A hand attached to a wrist control can still detach from a deformed fitted skin. If the forearm itself folds incorrectly, reconstruct the affected exposed arm through the evaluated sleeve edge instead of extending a rectangular wrist patch. Preserve the rest of the character. Plan palm/back/edge orientation for the action; an open outward-facing palm cannot stand in for an earpiece touch, phone grip, pointing finger or button press. For a large arm raise, interpolate joint angles or use a reachable arc so the wrist does not pass through the elbow and collapse the forearm. Inspect the approach and recovery as well as the held pose. Samtoons 002 v024's hands were explicitly rejected; [v025's record](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/002-how-arsenal-won-the-league/artifacts/hand-repair-v025/README.md) documents the subsequent reconstruction and its review limits. The user subsequently approved its scoped hand/movement fixes and the complete v026 cut on 21 September 2026; reuse that accepted construction for matching actions, while reviewing new views and gestures separately.

When isolating a hand or exposed forearm from a complete character mesh, bound the source region anatomically on both axes. A mask such as “left of x, below wrist y” can also select a shoe; hiding the hand then hides unrelated body parts. Inspect a full-body rendered frame while each replacement is active, as well as the wrist closeup. The replacement must meet the evaluated sleeve edge, preserve the wrist direction and use one visible outline where surfaces overlap. Evidence: Samtoons video 002, `artifacts/batch02-v017/hand-final-detail.png` and `point-fullbody-fixed.png`; this fixes a local selection/join defect, not all hand acting.

A fitted skin cannot reveal clothing that exists only underneath a painted glove or crossed arm. When the action separates that overlap, prepare source-matched articulated layers and the hidden garment surface before increasing gesture amplitude. Keep hand/finger pixels out of thigh and torso weights, bound masks against the actual silhouette, and remove unused transparent mesh regions where layered surfaces produce artifacts. Check the body after the hand moves away, not only the contact pose. Samtoons 002 v020 records these construction failures; the user rejected its resulting performance because the players still read as held action images.

Before adapting an action drawing into a continuous rig, check whether it can actually reach the required resting, preparation and recovery poses. If it cannot, reconstruct an appropriate whole-character base instead of adding more local controls. A neutral source does not replace matched drawings for views or extreme actions outside that rig's useful range. Check contacts against the visible boot/forehead surface, not a control placed inside the body; check the preceding and following encoded frames. For sideways shuffles, choose step length and leading foot so feet do not cross unintentionally. A successful geometry check is evidence of that constraint only, not creative acceptance.

### Reusing a shot without hidden dependencies

The [shared conditional clean-rebuild rule](../SKILL.md#rebuild-when-construction-is-the-root-cause) requires reconstruction only when a construction defect cannot be corrected reliably within the existing construction. Establish that need before overriding local repair/reuse advice. When necessary, rebuild the entire affected connected construction and its dependent data; do not transfer rejected joints, bindings or corrective patches into the replacement. Preserving the rest of a character is appropriate only when it is independent of the diagnosed failure.

When copying a rig or extending a shot into a new scene, remap parent, modifier, bone-constraint **and prop/shadow constraint** targets to the copied objects. Clearing keyframes alone does not freeze a constrained object. If a background is intentionally frozen, capture its evaluated world transform and remove its live dependency instead. Reload the saved scene and inspect the moving subject together with its contact shadow/held prop. Samtoons 002 v022 caught shadows still following the earlier scene during a camera pullback; the scoped builder and saved-scene check now cover those targets. This establishes a dependency check, not artistic approval of that revision.

For fitted raster skins, bound hand ownership by the source wrist and fingertips, including the surrounding mesh fringe; a hip-relative cutoff can assign long fingers or gloves to the legs and produce spikes during arm movement. Check both sides through the new action. Samtoons 002 v023 also caught inherited actor shadows inside a shared environment collection: reuse set geometry separately from actor-dependent shadows. Its detail corrections are recorded in the [final-batch review notes](../../productions/Samtoons/videos/002-how-arsenal-won-the-league/artifacts/final-batch-v023/README.md); they are scoped findings, not acceptance of the complete batch.
