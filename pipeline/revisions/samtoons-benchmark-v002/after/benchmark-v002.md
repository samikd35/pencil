## What to learn and how to prove it

Times are approximate source B playback times unless labelled A. The detailed study separates visible evidence from interpretation.

| Component | Reference observation | Original application and review proof |
|---|---|---|
| Story flow | B opening 8–2 boasting becomes 4–1 humiliation; post-match section continues the reversal | Define a character's expectation, successive reversals and final payoff. Every beat must change information, status or emotion; remove idle filler. |
| Character comedy | A repeated child treatment; B returning Müller reactions and changed circumstances | Select personas relevant to the new incident. Develop our own recurring gag with variation; never require fishing, babies or score signs in every film. |
| Football action | B ~14s, 21–26s, 56–57s and 75–76s: strike/contact/reaction are legible | Plan anticipation, support, contact, follow-through and recovery. Inspect consecutive frames around actual contact and watch normal-speed motion. Keep the underlying event and outcome verified. |
| Body construction | B selected native poses retain coherent silhouettes through action | Use connected anatomy fitted to each drawing/view. Check shoulder–elbow–wrist and hip–knee–ankle continuity, occlusion and planted feet. Control alignment alone cannot pass visual review. |
| Faces and acting | B ~2s, 80s, 85–90s and 118s use distinct expressions and group reactions | Plan gaze, face angle and expression for the story beat. Eyes must follow facial perspective without colliding with the nose; inspect close-ups and turn transitions at native size. |
| Timing | B mixes rapid impacts with held reactions; ~78s slowed-footage caption is an intentional gag | Give each action its own timing. A hold needs a comic/readability purpose. More in-betweens must improve motion rather than stretch an action. Review with the actual voice track. |
| Camera movement | B ~64–68s pullback; ~69–71s lateral reveal; ~76–78s rapid push-in/pullback | Name the attention shift that motivates each move and its start/end framing. Keep action readable; avoid a generic slow push on every shot. |
| Cuts and angles | B ~80–83s close-ups/action cuts; ~100s, 107s and 117s extreme-wide cuts | Plan coverage alongside action and sound. Distinguish a cut from camera travel. New angles require suitable drawings/views; do not expose flat artwork edge-on. |
| Environment | Pitch, net, seating, stage and interview spaces establish location with economical detail | Use a coherent perspective, scale and contact shadows; add story-relevant depth and props. Match environment complexity to framing, not a blanket detail quota. |
| Effects and props | B impact flashes, roses (~54s), SOS sign (~57s), trophy (~92s), interview sign (~106s) | Effects must clarify impact, emotion or a reveal. Anchor props to hands and effects to their causes. Design our own visual metaphors and ensure text reads at delivery size. |
| Voice and sound | B has an available source track, but performance details are unverified | Write emotion, intensity and timing per line; audition actual takes against picture. Shouts must sound performed, not merely louder. Preserve distinct personalities and approved takes. |
| Music and mix | No verified B music analysis; stream measurements are not a mastering target | If music is selected, use suitable free-to-use licensed material, retain attribution outside the picture, and duck it for speech. Listen to the complete mix; meters supplement listening. |
| Finish and presentation | B uses readable score/sign jokes and a separate end card | Keep internal production labels out of the film. Review transitions, frame edges, readable text, sync and delivery encoding. Do not copy mirrored kit lettering or other source defects. |

## Mandatory application in each new Samtoons video

Use the existing brief/shot plan, not another planning system. Consider **both A and B**, choose the moments relevant to this story, and record why a major component is not applicable. Do not force both sources into every shot.

Include this compact reference-application table before detailed animation:

| Source ID and time | Observed craft principle | Our original story/shot application | Planned action, camera and sound | Actual proof/version and review result or remaining gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B ~85–90s | Pull back from one reaction to reveal a group response | Describe the new episode's own reveal and why it is funny | Specify framing, action timing, reaction and sound cue | Fill with the rendered shot and concrete comparison, not “benchmark used” |

Select an appropriate native reference frame and motion interval for demanding shots. Compare their readability, connected construction, emotion, staging and timing with our **actual rendered output**, at comparable viewing size. References inform decisions; differences in visual identity are allowed by the brief. New character proportions, facing directions, actions and lines do not inherit approval from a different asset or scene.

### Apply through the existing four checkpoints

1. **Staging:** show chosen references beside original composition and critical action proof. Check character construction, facial perspective, screen direction, camera coverage and story clarity. Include the most demanding new pose/view before propagating its construction.
2. **Sound:** audition the actual performance and effects against the planned moment; record heard observations. Keep any unlistened source or output explicitly unverified. No approval based solely on loudness or captions.
3. **Animatic:** compare the whole film's escalation, timing, shot variety and joint picture/sound impact. Inspect critical movements frame by frame and at normal speed. Track every unresolved issue against its shot.
4. **Delivery:** review the exact final encoded picture and complete mix, including joins/end card. Confirm fixes survived assembly and no internal labels leaked into the film.

These are the shared pipeline's existing gates, not four additional approvals. User approval applies only to the shown version and scope. A visible broken limb, misaligned face, unreadable action or failed sound performance is not a cosmetic issue to defer under polish.

Snapshot this benchmark and selected supporting excerpts with the production context under the [existing convention](../README.md), recording versions/hashes. Update proof links as the film advances. Do not retroactively change completed films or their approvals.

## Originality and provenance

Borrow principles: escalation, readable contact, motivated framing, contrast and payoff. Create original scripts, caricatures, drawings, recordings, gags and compositions. Source frames and movies are private analysis evidence, not a production asset library. Verify real football events separately from the cartoon; its fictional dialogue, match clock and jokes are not historical evidence. No requirement to reproduce the same jokes, exact shot sequence, accents, music or creator identity.
