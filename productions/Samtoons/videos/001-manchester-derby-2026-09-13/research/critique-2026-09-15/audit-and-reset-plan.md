# Samtoons production audit and proposed reset

15 September 2026. Research and planning only. No new artwork, animation, sound, production script or workflow implementation was created for this audit. The user asked to examine the criticisms before restarting production.

## Judgment

The current treatment falls below the selected benchmark. Continuing to polish its portrait heads, generic bodies and front-on stadium would preserve the central problems. The failure is primarily design and production judgment: technical checks were allowed to stand in for a coherent visual treatment, accurate action staging and performed comedy.

Keep the selected story and useful motion mechanisms as inputs. Reopen the affected character/environment treatment, action staging and sound. Earlier acceptance of the opening is historical evidence of that specific review, not approval of every subsequent scene or sound.

## 1. Checkpoints were prescribed, but poorly applied

The skill specifies story/staging, sound direction, a complete combined animatic, and delivery playback. It also explicitly requires establishing the visual treatment before detailed character construction. Style belongs inside the existing staging review; it does not need a separate approval database.

The supplied 442oons reference settled the broad direction. That was a valid reason not to force the user through nineteen unrelated gallery choices. It did not settle how our own faces, bodies, stadium, goal and finish would realize that direction. The style-selection record says the user supplied the reference and selected the storyline. That is weaker evidence than a clearly presented, coherent, film-specific art treatment.

I narrowed review to the opening's neck connection and smoothness before resolving the whole design. The user really approved that revised opening. I should not treat that scoped approval as evidence that the full cast, other actions, environments or new audio meet the benchmark.

The current skill already says to show the reference beside a native film frame at comparable size and assess proportions, expressions, head/body coherence and whole-scene composition. The local benchmark explicitly rejects facial detail pasted onto a weaker body. The missing step is applying these requirements to the actual result; adding another framework will not solve it.

## 2. Environment and camera

The reference is economical, but its backgrounds explain space. In the 00:56.80 goal-area view, pitch markings, goal depth, keeper and striker share a readable perspective. The 00:38 touchline uses diagonal boards. The 01:48 dugout gives the reaction a distinct setting. Low-contrast crowd marks allow the characters to dominate.

Our repeated high-contrast crowd circles compete with the action. The goal is a flat rectangle/grid with no convincing side or rear depth. The figures and pitch markings do not establish comparable spatial relationships. An Old Trafford label does not supply those relationships.

Our shots largely repeat a frontal orthographic setup, with an ending pullback. The remedy is a deliberate shot plan: establish the play, show the decisive contact clearly, cut to the reaction, and reveal the next joke. Camera movement should serve those purposes. Constant movement or arbitrary zooms would not recreate the benchmark's clarity.

Recommended treatment: drawn stadium architecture and subdued crowd masses; consistent pitch perspective; a goal with visible side/top/back structure, plausible net scale and appropriate occlusion; ball and feet grounded with contact shadows. Use specific pitch, touchline and dugout compositions as the story needs them. A simple dimensional goal underneath the drawn finish is an option, not a requirement to rebuild the entire film in 3D.

## 3. Character design and smoothness

The existing heads have fine beard/hair detail and portrait-like facial modeling. The bodies use a much simpler, thinner construction. They appear to belong to different productions. Increasing body detail to chase the portraits would also miss the user's requested cartoon direction.

Design the complete character first: recognizable head shape, hair silhouette, facial features, build, posture, hands, kit and boots in one drawing language. Then derive compatible animation parts from that approved design. Use flat color and restrained shading, distinct player silhouettes, consistent outlines, and a deliberate jaw/neck/collar relationship.

Separate layers and replacement drawings are normal animation methods. Toon Boom describes mouth and hand substitutions as routine parts of cut-out animation; their existence is not the fault. Our fault is inconsistent artwork and insufficiently authored transitions. [Toon Boom: About Swapping Drawings](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-24/essentials/cut-out-animation/about-swap-drawing.html).

Use stable skull/eye/jaw positions, controllable eyes/brows/mouth, and matching alternate views. Draw action-specific poses and intermediate drawings when needed for turns, foreshortening, impacts and extreme expression. Do not replace the whole face with an independently proportioned portrait to make it act. Do not rely on crossfading portraits to conceal the mismatch.

The export frame rate and number of images are not measures of acting quality. Additional drawings must describe the intended movement. Preserve useful body motion where it suits the redesigned figures; revise the poses and transitions that do not.

## 4. Specific action errors

| Element | Evidence and cause | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Foden/Bruno | The source sequence includes a tussle, Foden going down, then a kick-out from the ground with Bruno nearby. Our pose starts from a simplified tilted/seated arrangement and a generic boot extension. | Board the approach, fall, kick-out, contact and reaction from adjacent source frames. Preserve the essential positioning and action before exaggerating the complaint or punishment. |
| Mbeumo/Donnarumma | The source shows the shot followed by an outstretched glove intervention and the ball leaving the keeper. Our ball follows a glove target and is pulled into a held catch. | Stage the actual parry: incoming path, contact, outgoing path, follow-through and landing. Correct the catch/hug assumption in the story treatment as well as the animation. Place the wolf gag after the legible save. |
| Ball | The current ball is a white circular disc with a central black pentagon and radial spokes. Its presentation reads as a symbol. | Use a consistent cartoon adaptation of the event's ball, appropriate size and panel treatment, visible spin when useful, a clear ground relationship and readable contact. |
| Tears | The code attaches streams to generic offsets on the head. Those offsets do not coincide with the drawn eye corners. | Anchor tears to the actual eyelids/eye corners for each view and expression. Let facial compression and cheek flow lead into the exaggerated fountain. |

One revealing failure: the numerical check measures how closely the captured ball follows its grip. It can report a perfect result for the wrong football action. The action must be established visually first; attachment checks only support it.

The user's permission for invented comic behavior remains intact. A crying apology, umbrella and fishing callback can work as editorial fantasy. That permission does not make a catch an adequate depiction of a parry.

## 5. Audio and dialogue

Choice B concerned the opening comparison: sparse foreground sound without the crowd bed. It did not approve all later effects or establish a final character-voice treatment.

The subsequent 28-second review has captions in place of spoken dialogue and six new analytically synthesized effect candidates. That may be a temporary timing aid; it is insufficient evidence for the benchmark's performed comedy. The current fountain is water-like synthesis, not a crying vocal performance. Calling a file a glove sound does not prove it sounds like glove contact.

Dean Stobbart describes his acting and voiceover background, voicing and singing work, and recording different characters in his first-person interview. This supports treating performance as central to the format. The interview is from 2018 and does not establish the tools or exact process used for the supplied video. [Creator interview, Reallusion](https://magazine.reallusion.com/2018/05/09/million-subscriber-youtuber-uses-crazytalk-for-sports-comedy-web-series/amp/).

Before timing detailed facial animation, establish an available route for convincing character dialogue and reactions. Audition actual short performances in context: Bruno's frustration, Foden's response, Oliver's escalating sob, Carrick's deadpan. Pair them with suitable sourced/recorded Foley for boot, ball, glove, turf and net, and selectively authored comic accents. Test the balance with and without ambience in representative match scenes. Do not extend an eight-second sound choice into a film-wide assumption.

No production-quality character-performance route has yet been demonstrated in this project. Resolve that dependency explicitly. System speech and arbitrary synthesized effects must not quietly become the final solution. If agent listening is unavailable, provide a real audition and identify the listening reviewer; keep unperformed listening unclaimed.

## Proposed sequence — awaiting the user's direction

The approximately 150-second selected film remains the production. These are its production materials, not a separate pilot.

1. **Visual treatment and staging.** Present two coherent execution choices within the settled recognizable-cartoon direction, with one recommendation. Each treatment board should show full-body design, a goal-area wide and an acting close-up at intended finish. Compare the recommended native treatment beside the benchmark. Include source-based key action poses for Foden and the parry before calling staging resolved. Settle this before extending the cast or polishing the film.
2. **Sound direction.** Present a representative excerpt with actual dialogue/reactions and credible Foley. Establish the voice production route first. Review meaningful sound alternatives in context. Combine this with the staging review if one artifact can answer both decisions clearly.
3. **Complete combined animatic.** Build the whole film with the actual cuts, readable action, reactions and selected performances. Review comedy, timing and continuity before detailed animation. Correct the two action errors while preserving the selected story's useful jokes.
4. **Animation, finishing and delivery.** Add the views, intermediate drawings, mouth shapes and action-specific poses the approved shots require. Inspect source contacts and expression transitions in motion. Finish the mix and review the exact encoded film with picture and sound.

Routine joint repairs, draw-order corrections and technical checks happen internally. They do not each become another user checkpoint. Every creative review should state the artifact, the decision being made, and what that decision unlocks.

Recommendation: start with the visual treatment package after the user reviews this plan. Keep Blender and existing useful helpers provisionally; changing software is not the first remedy for mismatched artwork, wrong action and absent vocal performance. Whether the approach meets the target must be demonstrated in the film's actual treatment, not promised from a tool name or drawing count.
