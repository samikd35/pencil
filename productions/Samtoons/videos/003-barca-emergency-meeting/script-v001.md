## S03 · 0:10–0:27 · We are absolutely not scared

**Picture.** A modest club meeting room. A large heading reads **HOW TO STOP BARCELONA — EMERGENCY MEETING**. Racing slumps over its seven-goal printout. Valencia points bitterly at the zero on **VALENCIA 0–5 BARCELONA**. Sevilla sits slightly straighter beside **SEVILLA 1–3 BARCELONA**. Two future opponents sit with clean notebooks; club identity is unnecessary for these silent roles.

Florentino Pérez, in his dark suit and glasses, strides to the head of the table. Chin high, controlled smile, one palm settling the room. His eyes check the doorway before his head follows. He locks the door and closes the curtains while delivering the line, then pushes a short cupboard across the door.

> **PÉREZ — smooth reassurance, stress “you”; strain creeps into the last word:** Relax. We're not scared. We're here to help **you**.

Valencia follows the cupboard with its eyes, then looks back at Madrid. It does not share Madrid's frantic movement.

> **VALENCIA — quiet, suspicious:** Then why did you book the room?

Madrid finishes the shove. A brief jaw clench; it straightens its clothes and ignores the question. Racing flinches at the lock, then relaxes when it understands the door is secure.

**Sound/camera.** Dry key click, curtain swish, short wood scrape. Medium-wide preserves Madrid's hands, feet, cupboard and door together. One reaction cut after the cupboard settles; no wandering camera during the line.

## S04 · 0:27–0:39 · Sevilla's tactical masterclass

Sevilla raises three fingers, cautiously proud. Madrid leans forward, suddenly hopeful. Valencia's pen hovers over a notebook.

> **SEVILLA — trying to find the bright side:** They only scored **three** against us.
>
> **PÉREZ — eager, confidence returning:** Good. How did you stop them?

Sevilla looks down for a beat, then meets Madrid's eyes.

> **SEVILLA — completely matter-of-fact:** We didn't. The match ended.

Madrid's smile hangs for a fraction too long before dying. Valencia puts the pen down without writing anything. Keep the reaction clean; no simultaneous gesturing by the whole room.

**Sound.** Music pauses before “The match ended.” Small pen contact after it. Cut on Madrid's decision to take over.

## S05 · 0:39–0:46 · The brilliant plan

Madrid stands at a tactics board headed **HOW TO BEAT BARCELONA**. It circles **RAPHINHA**, then pushes three opposing markers into a tight cluster around him. A large empty channel remains visible on the other side.

> **PÉREZ — brisk command, recovering status:** Simple. Stop Raphinha. Put everyone on him.

Sevilla studies the empty channel. Racing eagerly copies the crowded diagram instead.

**Transition.** Match the board's player positions to the training-ground wide. The real drill answers this precise proposal; it is not an unrelated scoring montage.

## S06 · 0:46–0:59 · You forgot somebody

**Picture.** Barça's training ground operates like a factory: real marked turf, a regulation-proportioned goal, a ball-return belt outside the playing lane and a simple goal counter. The players do the football.

Three practice defenders shuffle inward to surround Raphinha. His annoyed glance notices Yamal alone to screen-right. The expression becomes an amused half-smile; he points into the empty channel with an unmistakable index finger.

> **RAPHINHA — amused challenge:** Who's watching **him**?

Pedri scans once, plants his support foot and passes through the opening. Yamal receives in stride, takes a touch, plants and finishes. The defenders turn late, individually, following the already-departing ball. Raphinha slips out of the crowd to collect the next ball.

**Camera/mechanics.** Establish all four roles in one oblique wide: decoy, passer, receiver, defenders. Maintain left-to-right attack through the closer finish. Show both foot contacts, the continuous ball trajectory and the goal crossing. Keep the goalkeeper beyond the finish frame for the following voice plant; do not stage an empty goal as if a goalkeeper made a save.

**Sound.** Stud scuff → pass contact → reception → strike → net. One short counter click after the ball crosses the line. No counter increment on the kick itself.

## S07 · 0:59–1:13 · An ordinary working day

An assistant approaches Flick, pleased with the result sheet marked **7**. Flick finishes marking his clipboard before he looks up.

> **ASSISTANT — hopeful, asking permission:** We scored seven. Can we slow down now?
>
> **FLICK — calm, no raised voice; stress “did” and “only”:** We **did** slow down. That's why it was **only** seven.

The assistant's smile fades. Offscreen, the same goalkeeper voice used in the ending asks:

> **BARÇA KEEPER, OFFSCREEN — exhausted:** Can we have a break?

Flick checks his watch, puts down the clipboard, bends and rolls another ball into play. The assistant silently shuts its mouth. Cut on the ball leaving Flick's fingers.

**Sound.** Let the return belt and distant football continue quietly. A short pocket of silence follows “seven”; no joke-explaining sting. The roll/contact is audible. The keeper remains unseen.

## S08 · 1:13–1:31 · Call the experts

Back in the meeting, Madrid crosses out the crowded plan. It spots an old Bayern result clipping and reaches for the laptop.

> **PÉREZ — sudden inspiration:** Call Bayern. They beat Barcelona eight–two. They'll know what to do.

Video call connects. Thomas Müller, in a casual top with a Bayern scarf, sits proudly beside a framed **BAYERN 8–2 BARCELONA · 2020** picture. A second frame faces the wall. Madrid's face is in a small call tile so Bayern's eye-line has a definite target.

> **MÜLLER — relishing the memory:** Eight goals. A very good night.
>
> **PÉREZ — direct, expecting an answer:** Good. How do we stop Flick doing that to **us**?

Müller's eyes stop on “Flick.” The proud smile tightens; it lowers the picture. Hold the change before the next line. Madrid already knows Flick's Bayern history; no false discovery gag.

## S09 · 1:31–1:47 · Ask the former employee

Müller hurriedly adds Lewandowski to the call. The screen layout expands visibly; Robert does not materialize in the meeting room. A short, readable label introduces **ROBERT LEWANDOWSKI · FORMER BAYERN / BARÇA**.

> **MÜLLER — passing responsibility:** Robert played for both clubs. Ask him.
>
> **PÉREZ — genuine request beneath the command:** Robert, how do we make Flick stop scoring?
>
> **LEWANDOWSKI — relaxed correction:** Wrong question. He spent years telling **me** to score more.

Robert sets down his cup before answering. Madrid waits with its pen ready; Bayern avoids Robert's gaze. Cut when Robert glances toward his old Bayern photo.

## S10 · 1:47–1:58 · Same boss, different shirt

**Picture.** Clearly introduced memory: **BAYERN · 2020**. Lewandowski finishes a practice chance in a Bayern shirt. Flick immediately rolls in another ball. Match cut on that rolling ball to **BARCELONA · NOW**: same directional action, Raphinha receives the next one. Keep the training action clearly illustrative, not claimed match footage.

> **LEWANDOWSKI, OVER THE MEMORY — lightly amused:** At Bayern, he told us to keep scoring against Barcelona. Now he's at Barcelona, telling them to score against everyone else.

Flick's unchanged expression is the visual answer. Avoid adding dialogue or a second goal-count joke here.

## S11 · 1:58–2:11 · The other photograph

Back on the call, Robert notices Bayern's reversed frame.

> **LEWANDOWSKI — pleasantly inconvenient:** Show them the other picture.
>
> **MÜLLER — defensive, too quick:** That's not relevant.
>
> **LEWANDOWSKI — unhurried:** The one where Barcelona scored **four** against you.

Müller slowly turns it: **BARCELONA 4–1 BAYERN · 2024**. Its confident upright posture folds inward. Madrid's pen stops. Allow the result to be read before cutting away.

**Sound.** Frame scrape, then room tone; let Bayern's discomfort carry the punchline.

## S14 · 2:36–2:54 · The expert

At the established factory desk, Flick looks at the ringing phone, puts the clipboard down and grips the handset. His hand closes around it before the receiver lifts; the earpiece rests at the ear, palm toward the head.

> **FLICK — ordinary professional greeting:** Hello. Hansi Flick speaking.

Cut to the meeting. Madrid's eyes widen before the head retracts. Sevilla's pen drops. Racing freezes mid-sip. Valencia slowly turns to stare at Madrid. **About one second with no dialogue**; reactions have distinct timings.

>
> **FLICK — recognizes the caller, entirely sincere:** Madrid? Good. We're getting ready for you.

Behind Flick, Raphinha opens a box marked **REAL MADRID**, places a ball inside, looks up at the phone and adds another. Flick barely notices. Madrid abruptly ends the call. No wild camera shake.
