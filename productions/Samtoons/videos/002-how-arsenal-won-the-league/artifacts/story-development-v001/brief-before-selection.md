## Step 1 — choose the main story approach

All three options retain the rival perspective, Arsenal as the subject of the roast, full fictional freedom and the season-long journey. These are concrete alternatives, not three films to produce.

### A. Bitter narrator, player-led sketches — recommended

A rival narrates Arsenal's season with increasing disbelief. His insults become acted scenes featuring Arteta, the players, Pep and a fictional VAR character. Players can interrupt the narration and answer back. Most screen time belongs to the cartoon action rather than a fan at a desk.

**Sample scene, approximate 12–15 seconds:**

| Beat | Picture and focus | Dialogue / sound / performance |
|---|---|---|
| A01 | Gyökeres strides into a mock unveiling, expecting applause. Begin medium-wide to establish him and Arteta. | Narrator, knowingly dismissive: “First, Arsenal needed a striker.” Brief applause. |
| A02 | Cut to Arteta enthusiastically rolling out a red carpet. Reveal the carpet ends at a corner flag wearing the number nine shirt. Gyökeres stands abandoned to one side. | Applause swells for the flag; abruptly stop it as we cut to Gyökeres. |
| A03 | Gyökeres looks from the flag to Arteta. Hold a two-shot for the reply. | Gyökeres, offended: “Then why am I here?” Arteta, completely sincere: “Win us a corner.” Protect a short silent reaction after “corner.” |

**Why it fits:** easy to combine season milestones with completely fictional sketches; gives players comic agency. The narrator supplies rivalry, not continuous factual exposition. Later chapters can include the City chase and a VAR rescue without changing the whole film's format.

**Main risk:** too much voiceover. Each sketch needs a visible action or exchange that advances the joke.

### B. Arsenal's season as a survival movie

The whole season becomes an absurd obstacle course. Arteta tries to transport a fragile league trophy to May while Pep hunts him. Rival-fan commentary cheers every danger. Defensive walls, corner-launchers and a VAR rescue become tools the Arsenal cast physically uses.

**Sample scene:** Arteta whispers, “Nobody panic.” A tiny notification sounds: “City have a game in hand.” He immediately straps the trophy to his chest and jumps through a window. Cut wide to reveal Gabriel and Saliba already waiting below with a mattress. Their sigh lands before the impact.

**Why it fits:** strongest sustained physical action and the clearest version of “running from City for their life.”

**Main risk:** the metaphor can swallow the football jokes. The threats and escapes should remain recognizably Arsenal-specific.

### C. The rival-fan emergency meeting

A collection of rival fans convenes to explain how Arsenal's title was allowed to happen. Their increasingly ridiculous testimony becomes animated flashbacks. They blame corners, defending, Pep and VAR, then turn on each other.

**Sample scene:** a United fan announces, “Nobody leaves until we agree this title doesn't count.” A Spurs fan raises a hand: “Can we agree corners are cheating?” Cut to an Arsenal training pitch where Arteta peels the centre circle off the turf and replaces it with four more corners.

**Why it fits:** strongest fan-versus-fan dialogue and opportunities for escalating contradictions.

**Main risk:** meeting-room repetition. Testimony must quickly become active football sketches, with distinct roles for each fan.

**Current recommendation:** A as the main format. It can accommodate a short chase or coalition scene as an individual sketch while keeping the season easy to follow. Narrator club identity remains open; choosing A does not automatically select a United fan.

**Decision requested:** Which main approach feels right: A, B or C? The user may also describe a specific combination or a different direction. No option has been selected yet.

## Subsequent interactive steps

1. Choose the main approach above.
2. Shape the narrator/cast and opening with a short dialogue-and-action excerpt.
3. Choose the major seasonal chapters and recurring joke/payoff; remove weak material.
4. Write and revise one sequence at a time, including performance, picture and sound.
5. Assemble and read through the complete 3–4-minute audiovisual script, then revise pacing and ending together.

Only step 1 is currently being presented for a choice. Detailed animation and its existing staging/sound/animatic/delivery checkpoints follow when that work is authorized. No new approval framework or production tooling is introduced.
