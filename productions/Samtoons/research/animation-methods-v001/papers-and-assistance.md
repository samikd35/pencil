# Research methods and assistance for Samtoons

Researched 23 September 2026 against primary papers, authors’ project pages, official repositories and Blender documentation. Read alongside [the Blender/Python practice note](../python-animation-practice-v001.md) and [the earlier seam diagnosis](../../../../research/seamless-cartoon-from-stickman.md). Research only: no models downloaded, software installed, animation generated or film assets changed. Published demonstrations are evidence of the authors’ results; none was reproduced on this Mac.

**Finding:** There are credible alternatives to deforming one flat picture. The strongest routes either make a 3D proxy that can turn, or generate new raster drawings from authored sketch/pose guidance. Editable-vector methods retain useful geometry but mostly move the artwork already supplied. None of the inspected systems establishes a finished, editable football performance with recognizable player likeness, intentional hand shapes and dependable ball/prop contact from one reference plus an action prompt.

The three most relevant candidates below have different jobs; this is a ranking of useful investigations, not a recommendation to install three pipelines.

## 1. DrawingSpinUp: a real 3D proxy and controllable motion

**Demonstrated.** DrawingSpinUp (SIGGRAPH Asia 2024) removes drawn contours before multi-view reconstruction, makes a textured 3D character, retargets 3D motion, then restores view-dependent drawn contours with a learned renderer. Its study covers 120 amateur character drawings. This specifically addresses turns and motion out of the original drawing plane. The paper explains why treating a black silhouette line as a surface texture damages ordinary image-to-3D reconstruction. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08615v1), [authors’ examples and limitations](https://lordliang.github.io/DrawingSpinUp/).

**Actual Blender route.** The official implementation produces an OBJ, uses Mixamo to obtain a rigged FBX, and allows motion retargeting through Rokoko’s Blender add-on. It renders in Blender before the final neural stylization. Thus the **proxy mesh, rig and animation** are editable; the final stylized outlines are **raster output**, not an exported Grease Pencil rig. The documented renderer is Blender 3.6.14; compatibility with this project’s 5.2 build is untested. [Official implementation](https://github.com/LordLiang/DrawingSpinUp).

**Limits and local feasibility.** The published contour stage can introduce artifacts with thick input outlines or unsuitable edge thresholds. Its evidence does not establish expressive facial controls, articulated fingers, accurate player likeness or contact solving. The full supplied stack targets Ubuntu/CUDA, PyTorch and tiny-cuda-nn; experiments used an RTX 4090, with 2080 Ti also reported usable. It trains a stylization model for each character. This is **not a supplied Apple Silicon installation path**. No top-level code license was visible in the inspected repository; the project website’s content license is not a code license. [Project limitations](https://lordliang.github.io/DrawingSpinUp/), [setup and workflow](https://github.com/LordLiang/DrawingSpinUp).

**Samtoons judgment.** This is the strongest research evidence for the architecture “editable 3D movement underneath a drawn appearance.” A small, deliberately modeled head/body proxy with authored facial drawings could use that architecture without importing this entire research stack. That simpler adaptation is our proposal, not a tested DrawingSpinUp feature. The key uncertainty is whether the user prefers its turning silhouette and likeness to authored 2D replacement poses.

## 2. ToonComposer: redraw between explicit sketch keyframes

**Demonstrated.** ToonComposer (official repository now identifies ICLR 2026) accepts color reference keyframes, sparse sketches at selected frames, text and optional motion masks, and generates inbetweened, colored video. Unlike a mesh warp, it can synthesize visual content between different key drawings. Its release uses Wan2.1 I2V 14B; the documented example of 61 frames at 480p requires about **57 GB VRAM**. The authors explicitly warn that output varies with seeds, settings and inputs. [Official repository](https://github.com/TencentARC/ToonComposer), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10881).

**Actual Blender route.** Author poses, silhouettes, timing and ball positions as Blender/Grease Pencil sketches; pass selected rendered frames to the model; bring returned raster frames into Blender for compositing or draw-over reference. No rig, separated hands or editable vector animation exporter is documented. Editing a generated elbow or facial feature means repainting frames or changing conditioning and regenerating, not moving a reliable control.

**Limits and local feasibility.** Sparse guidance is not proof of exact contact on the intervening frames. A recognizable head turning still needs useful key drawings and inspection of likeness. The repository defaults to CUDA and documents no validated MPS workflow; a CPU device option is not evidence of practical Mac performance. Its code and released weights are MIT, with Wan2.1 components under Apache-2.0. [Runtime/setup](https://github.com/TencentARC/ToonComposer), [license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TencentARC/ToonComposer/main/LICENSE).

**Samtoons judgment.** Strongest candidate for a *bounded assistance test* after the important drawings and timing exist: one short turn or reaction, compared against a manually controlled version. It should not own the only master of a kick, save, handoff or other incident-defining action.

## 3. Monster Mash: simple editable volume without a diffusion model

**Demonstrated.** Monster Mash (SIGGRAPH Asia 2020) stitches and inflates sketched regions into a soft 3D surface. Its ARAP-L deformation uses layering constraints to handle visible overlaps, and the user records control-point movements. It therefore offers actual volume and deformation rather than a collection of rigid flat pieces. The soft, floppy result is a design property, not physically accurate anatomy. [Creators’ technical account](https://research.google/blog/monster-mash-a-sketch-based-tool-for-casual-3d-modeling-and-animation/).

**Actual Blender route.** Its official demo can export a textured animated glTF, explicitly reported tested in Blender, or an individual OBJ frame. Import the geometry/animation and refine it there. Exported motion is not a promise of a clean animator-friendly armature. [Official help and export description](https://monstermash.zone/).

**Limits and local feasibility.** The browser demo requires WebAssembly/WebGL 1.0 and sufficient computing resources, so it has the clearest documented platform-neutral local route here; Apple Silicon performance has not been tested in this work. The method starts with drawn regions rather than automatically recovering the identity details in a caricature. Face shape, hands, controlled feet and football contact remain authoring tasks. The source is Apache-2.0, but third-party code can have other terms; the repository specifically flags Triangle. The hosted demo describes evaluation/research use. [Official requirements](https://monstermash.zone/), [source and dependency notices](https://github.com/google/monster-mash).

**Samtoons judgment.** Useful as a quick proxy-building experiment if seamless volume is the question. It is less convincing as the final player model than a few carefully modeled forms, and does not itself solve the missing expression/hand/view drawings. Do not add it merely to replace a primitive Blender can already construct.

## What transfers into the existing workflow

Blender documents SVG import into Grease Pencil and glTF import of geometry and animation. These are credible asset bridges; they do not guarantee that a research system’s entire representation or controls survive the transfer. Sequential SVG frames still require explicit frame assignment and cleanup. [Blender 5.2 SVG documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/nb/5.2/files/import_export/grease_pencil_svg.html), [Blender glTF documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/id/5.0/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html).

**Research inference for Samtoons:** use a representation that actually contains the required view, pose, hand shape and expression. Choose between authored replacement drawings and a designed 3D proxy where turns require them; let Python manage timing, switches, deformation, contacts and export. Generative assistance is easiest to evaluate after those decisions are explicit. Raster-to-vector tracing cannot recover the intended rig, contact logic or identity decisions that a generated frame omitted.

A discriminating later proof would combine one head/body turn, a hand changing shape around a held prop, and a planted-foot-to-ball action. Compare likeness, silhouette continuity, contact and the cost of one requested correction. A pretty isolated loop or a successful import is not evidence that the production problem is solved. This is a proposed evaluation, not work performed here.
