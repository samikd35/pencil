## Reusable findings, grounded in observed pictures

1. The environment is economical but not blank: patterned crowd band, advertising strips, geometric mowing patches, goal posts/net, white pitch lines, bench shelter and seats, tunnel, whiteboard. Repeated backdrops establish continuity while different frontal/oblique angles and scales refresh staging.
2. Humor often has a visible chain: prop premise → cut-in → reply prop; kick → absurd collision → ground recovery; successful shot → conspicuously late dive; optical POV → cut to binocular holder → long pass; composed passer → shower of flowers. These require planned insert and reaction coverage, not arbitrary camera movement.
3. Acting varies by shot purpose: dialogue-like portraits favor changing eyes/mouth and one gesturing arm, running uses repeated complete strides and body translation, impacts use bursts plus an abrupt trajectory change, recovery uses elbows/knees/hands. Deliberate holds buy legibility around gags; this reference is not constant full-body movement.
4. Thick dark contour, flat high-saturation kits, distinct hair/face silhouettes, oversized shoes, simple articulated limbs and minimally shaded bodies. Faces can be much more detailed than bodies. Different player statures are plainly visible. Exact rendering construction cannot be derived from screenshots.
5. Effects are sparse and functional: small yellow radial bursts at ball/body contact, red selection pointer, binocular/circular masks, placards/signs and flowers. They supply cause or joke; no evidence here for constant decorative particles.
6. Cuts, pans and scale moves must be logged separately. A stable scene with player crossing is not a pan. Gradual whole-scene enlargement/reduction here is observable; whether produced by a camera, compositing scale or layer transforms is unknowable from frames alone. No source-rig claim is justified.
