## How it may have been constructed — separate evidence from inference

**Observed:** coordinated changing facial shapes, purpose-specific body silhouettes, recurring character designs and useful overlapping/occluding bodies.

**Plausible implementation:** reusable layered character assets with keyed transforms, facial controls/drawing substitutions, and additional poses for actions that exceed a resting drawing. The output is compatible with this approach, but pixels alone cannot identify the authoring software or prove which parts were rigged versus redrawn.

**Historical primary-source context:** Dean Stobbart described 442oons' early workflow as CrazyTalk Animator for animation, Flash for art/SWF creation and MoviePlus for titles/transitions in a [Reallusion interview](https://magazine.reallusion.com/2018/05/09/million-subscriber-youtuber-uses-crazytalk-for-sports-comedy-web-series/). The article is dated 2018 but discusses an early period of the channel. This establishes that a reusable cut-out workflow can underlie the benchmark creator's work; it does NOT confirm the supplied Short's original authorship, the software used for it, or current 442oons tooling. Original-source candidates were not visually matched and are therefore not treated as attribution.

Official [Toon Boom teaching](https://learn.toonboom.com/modules/layout-and-posing/topic/about-swapping-drawings1?c=32) supports combining transformed parts with additional drawings. [Animation Mentor's facial acting guidance](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/facial-animation-101/) supports constructing meaningful expression/attention changes. These are methods to implement comparable capabilities, not evidence that this clip used those products.

## Difference from Samtoons video 002

The essential difference is the expressive vocabulary and its use. Our inspected shared helpers frequently deform a single rest view, add mouth/blink activity and return to that rest. This reference offers seated poses, head-holding, clenched celebration, sporting challenges and differentiated facial attitudes. Some of its heads also remain at a recurring angle, so 'turn every head more often' would be an overcorrection. The goal is the appropriate expression, view and body pose for the event.

Likewise, a frozen frame can be excellent animation staging. The issue is whether the surrounding sequence earns the hold and changes meaning when needed. Adding constant sway, random eye darts or larger gestures would not reproduce the reference's strengths.

## Cast-independent next-production workflow

This workflow applies to whichever characters and actions are actually selected for the next video. It requires neither Arteta nor Gyökeres, nor a remake of video 002.

1. **After the new idea is selected, identify its visual demands.** In the existing shot plan, note consequential reactions, looks/turns, unusual poses, contacts and camera views. Select reference excerpts by the same action type rather than by player name.
2. **Block the scene with rough, coherent drawings.** Establish pose, expression, look target, screen direction and contact through the intended camera before cleaning artwork. Include listeners and opponents where their reactions matter.
3. **Derive the asset requirements from that scene.** Reuse compatible identity and controls. Add only the needed views, expressions, hand shapes and whole-body poses. Construct neck, torso and limb overlap together; avoid independently styled heads pasted onto bodies.
4. **Use the appropriate motion method per action.** Deform articulated native drawings for supported movement. Use matched replacement drawings/breakdowns when view, silhouette or occlusion changes cannot be achieved convincingly with that deformation. A new drawing is useful for ordinary acting too, not only extreme running/jumping.
5. **Author timing and interaction.** Coordinate attention, expression, body response and recovery. For football, coordinate support foot, ball contact, opponents and aftermath. Let stillness remain where it communicates the intention.
6. **Use a real excerpt from the new production as the proof.** Choose the smallest excerpt exposing its important risks. An interaction proof and a demanding-action proof may be different excerpts. Duration and cast follow the actual project; there is no compulsory eight-second scene or named-player test.
7. **Review the complete performance before replication.** Compare against relevant reference excerpts and the accepted baseline at intended viewing size. Inspect normal-speed playback, then intermediate frames where needed. Verify likeness, continuity, readable reaction and contact together. Record actual review coverage and unknowns.
8. **Preserve proven assets, not generic choreography.** Record reusable controls, view drawings and tested limits in the existing Samtoons records. New shots still require their own acting decisions. No new framework/database or arbitrary number of expression assets is required.

## What changes in Codex–Blender responsibilities

Codex should author a shot-specific pose/expression/contact plan and construct missing geometry or drawing variants when needed. Blender holds editable art, controls, drawing exposure, cameras and timing. Automation handles setup, repeated transforms, rendering and measurable checks. The artistic choices cannot be replaced by one generic gesture function, and converting an image to Grease Pencil strokes does not invent missing acting.

The immediate action is to carry these observed capabilities into planning the next production, then prove them on that production's chosen excerpt before extending its cast. A finished-film revision or a mandatory old-character experiment is not implied. No production assets were changed in this study.
