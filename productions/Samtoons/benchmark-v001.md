# Samtoons benchmark v001

**Proposed reusable production standard · 14 September 2026**

Create recognizable, expressive football caricature comedy: a verified incident, one clear comic idea, readable action, escalating consequences and a complete ending. Use simple construction to support strong acting. Samtoons should develop its own artwork, writing, voices and presentation.

This proposal derives from the [442oons reference study](research/reference-analysis.md), the [verified event/creator research](research/source-context.md), and Pencil's existing production capabilities. The numerical targets below are **Samtoons planning and review criteria**, not measured 442oons rules or evidence of audience performance. The user has requested this benchmark; its detailed creative choices have not yet been accepted for a particular film.

## The standard to aim for

| Area | Minimum acceptable result | Reference anchor / review |
|---|---|---|
| Story promise | Identify the event or situation and the main tension within the opening 0–3 seconds. Skip a separate logo introduction. | The source opens on the matchup and result. A viewer should know what the film is about without reading its title. |
| Comic structure | One main comic premise; establish it, vary it, heighten the consequence, then resolve it. A repeated action must change meaning or outcome. | Offside → repetition → absurd awards/injury → later callbacks. Copy the mechanism, not those jokes. |
| Factual integrity | Preserve the important participants, action sequence and actual outcome. Keep a short fact/parody distinction in the brief. | Verify official reports, scores and dates independently of the cartoon. |
| Likeness | Each main figure has at least three intentional distinguishing cues across face, hair, silhouette, posture or costume; use suitable real-person references. | A face/kit/pose comparison at intended viewing size. Repeated generic heads fail. |
| Unified artwork | Face, neck, body, hands, kit and boots use a coherent outline and fill treatment. Connections survive the selected poses. | Compare a representative finished frame with the source board. Facial detail must not look pasted onto a weaker body. |
| Expressions and hands | Supply the expressions, gaze directions, mouth shapes and hand poses demanded by the actual script. Each major reaction visibly changes the acting. | Review neutral → expectation → reversal/reaction. Do not create an unused expression library. |
| Action and contact | Show anticipation, action, contact or release, follow-through and a readable result where the shot needs them. Plant feet during weight-bearing holds. | Inspect the film's actual hardest action at normal speed and in adjacent frames. No ball teleporting or limbs separating. |
| Economical motion | Hold supporting figures when that helps attention; use purposeful eye, face, hand or body changes for active figures. | A held pose must remain intentional. Automatic bobbing does not count as acting. |
| Composition | One dominant action/read per shot; supporting characters remain identifiable. Keep the relevant hand, ball, face and outcome inside the delivery frame. | Check the first frame, peak action and result in the actual aspect ratio. |
| Edit and pacing | Each cut reveals an action, response or new piece of information. Leave time to register the setup and payoff. | Start with roughly 6–10 shots for 32–60 seconds, then change the count to fit the story. No mandatory cut-every-N-seconds rule. |
| Sound and performance | A sound intention accompanies every story beat; chosen sounds and any speech are auditioned with the picture. Punchlines and key words remain intelligible. | Source listening remains unverified. Use the film's own sound and combined-animatic reviews. |
| Finish | Review the actual encoded movie for readability, motion, sync, cut-off audio, clipping and final story clarity. | A technically valid render is necessary but cannot establish that the joke works. |

## A shorter story shape

Use **45–55 seconds** as the first-film working recommendation within the existing 32–60-second target. Choose the aspect ratio in the brief: **9:16 is recommended for a Short**, while the source is 16:9. Re-stage for vertical; a center crop of the wide defensive line would lose necessary participants.

The following is a 48-second example allocation, not a script or approved first subject:

| Time | Audience experience | Picture and sound planning |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Understand the situation immediately. | Strong first pose/action, brief identifying context and an intentional opening sound. |
| 3–11 s | Learn what the character wants and the obstacle. | Show the relationship or football action that makes the joke understandable. |
| 11–23 s | Recognize a repeated pattern with a meaningful variation. | Change the response, timing or pose; sound can reinforce recognition. |
| 23–36 s | See the consequence grow or expectation reverse. | Spend the strongest new action/prop here. Keep the factual sequence intact. |
| 36–45 s | Get the decisive outcome and main payoff. | Give the result and reaction room; prioritize intelligibility over another joke. |
| 45–48 s | Receive a final callback and closure. | Finish the story and preserve the sound tail; branding must not delay the ending. |

If the chosen event cannot be told clearly within this shape, narrow its focus or deliberately choose another runtime. Do not speed everything up to force a fit. A full four-goal recap and several elaborate subplots are a different scope from one incident.

## How to judge every video

Use this rubric inside the existing staging, sound, animatic and delivery reviews. It adds no separate approval system. Record the reviewed artifact/version, viewing conditions, score, observed issue and next correction in the video's journal.

Scoring anchors: **0** absent/broken; **1** major problems; **2** understandable but noticeably weak; **3** clear and production-ready at the agreed scope; **4** especially strong execution demonstrated in the reviewed artifact. **U** means unreviewed, not zero and not a pass.

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence required for a 3 |
|---|---:|---|
| Story and comic payoff | 25 | One-view retelling captures setup, change and ending; the joke does not need an explanation afterward. |
| Character design and likeness | 20 | Main figures remain distinct at phone size across the actual poses, with coherent head/body artwork. |
| Acting, movement and contact | 20 | Actions read continuously; major reactions are specific; visible contacts and attachments hold. |
| Staging and edit | 15 | Attention follows the intended action across cuts; the final crop preserves essential information. |
| Sound and audiovisual timing | 15 | Actual listening confirms intelligibility, fitting sound identity, aligned events and an effective payoff. |
| Delivery finish | 5 | The encoded movie plays and decodes correctly and has been reviewed at delivery size with sound. |

Calculate `sum(weight × score / 4)`. The proposed release floor is **75/100 with every dimension at least 3 and no U**. Thus excellence in one area cannot compensate for a weak or unreviewed area. An 85+ result is a stretch aspiration after the first production proves the method. Scores support reviewer judgment; they do not predict views or acceptance by the user.

The following block release regardless of score: incorrect essential facts; an unreadable decisive action; character/prop disconnection during an important contact; lost words or punchline due to the mix; unintended missing/black frames; or an incomplete ending. Fix the specific defect and revisit only the affected reviews.

Practical review passes:

1. **One viewing, no coaching:** ask a reviewer to state who wanted what, what changed and how it ended. Record their actual response; do not invent a comprehension pass.
2. **Small display and mute:** verify principal figures and essential physical cause/effect. If the joke uses dialogue, its verbal meaning need not survive mute; the visual action still should.
3. **Normal speed, then targeted frames:** assess the performance first; use frame stepping to diagnose ball/hand/foot contact and overlap defects.
4. **Sound with picture:** audition on headphones and an ordinary small speaker at a comfortable level. Judge performance and mix, not only loudness numbers.
5. **Final encode:** confirm the actual output, including first/last frames, all cuts, sound tails and selected specifications. Acceptance of an earlier preview does not prove the encode is correct.

## Production plan before invoking the skill

The reference research is complete to the evidence limits stated in the analysis. The next production should proceed through the existing workflow once its subject and brief are set:

1. **Choose the first real incident.** Record date, participants, important actions, outcome and primary sources. A possible first subject is the offside frustration in this same Clásico, using the verified 0–4 outcome and original comic treatment. This is a candidate, not a selection; the supplied URL was a style reference.
2. **Write a one-page brief.** State the comic premise, intended audience, 32–60-second duration, aspect ratio, tone, and whether it uses dialogue, vocal reactions or primarily visual humor. Keep its ending complete.
3. **Write picture and sound together.** Each beat specifies action, emotion, sound purpose and required factual information. Start with two or three principal speaking/acting figures, one main environment and only the supporting characters/props the real action needs. This is a scope recommendation, not permission to erase essential participants.
4. **Design for the actual shots.** Prepare the required character views, expressions and props. Compare a finished representative frame against this board. Use the film's hardest action for a short motion proof, rather than using an unrelated historical scene as the visual standard.
5. **Review the complete inexpensive animatic.** Check the story, performance and sound together before full-quality rendering. Apply the existing staging, sound and animatic checkpoint rules with their actual scope and reviewer.
6. **Finish and evaluate.** Render after the applicable reviews, inspect the encoded film, record the rubric results and archive the exact selected assets. Use the first film's actual effort and defects to improve estimates for the next one.

At the transition into production, invoke the authoritative [pipeline/SKILL.md](../../pipeline/SKILL.md). The archived guide linked in the request is historical. This planning task has not invoked the production skill, created a film scene or accepted a creative checkpoint.

## What can be reused, and what needs proof

Pencil already has native flat artwork, editable controls, continuous limb attachment methods, foreground hand/sleeve layering, versioned style selection, sound-event alignment and per-film journals. See the [construction guide](../../pipeline/styles/construction.md), [caricature recipe](../../pipeline/styles/recipes/caricature.md) and [pipeline execution map](../../pipeline/README.md#where-the-proven-mechanisms-live).

Those are useful mechanisms. They do not yet establish a Samtoons cast, source-level facial acting, lip sync, every camera angle or the new film's comic timing. Inspect compatibility and add only the missing artwork or action support demanded by the chosen film. Preserve existing films and experiments.

The catalogue's caricature option is adjacent to this direction, but its still image is a different artist's work. Do not let a preset label replace the selected 442oons craft reference or automatically pin that catalogue artwork as the user's selection. Adopt this benchmark and relevant evidence explicitly in the video brief/assets.

Reuse compatible characters, expressions, views, stadium modules and licensed sounds once a real film has tested them. Author each new performance. The creator's historical asset-reuse examples support this approach but do not justify an eight-hour production promise here.

After the first accepted film, preserve v001 and write a new benchmark version only if actual production evidence or user direction warrants changes. No production database, generic rig framework or publishing automation is required for this stage.
