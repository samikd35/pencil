# Samtoons — production profile

Profile v005 · maintained 22 September 2026. Samtoons is the user's continuing **football-comedy** production. Its recurring format is authorized; each video should work independently. Shared plot continuity and continuing fictional arcs have not been selected.

This profile supplies defaults for new work. Existing videos retain their adopted context, assets, decisions and explicit exceptions. The [shared pipeline](../../pipeline/SKILL.md) owns the production steps and four reviews; Samtoons owns the creative direction below. Other productions do not inherit these preferences automatically.

## Audience and creative promise

Original animated football comedy for fans: recognizable football personalities, readable situations, contrasting attitudes, escalating reactions and a clear payoff. Research relevant incidents, personas and useful fan jokes, then create original scenes. Avoid turning one episode's jokes into a recurring formula.

The default incident-based treatment verifies essential actions, participants and outcome while freely exaggerating dialogue and visual comedy. An explicitly fictional brief can depart from that default: video 002 authorized invented season satire. Record the chosen treatment in each new brief; neither that exception nor another video's verified premise silently governs the next story. Distinguish parody from factual allegations in the appropriate publication context, without unnecessary internal labels inside the entertainment picture.

## Visual target

**Expressive characters + readable exaggerated poses + excellent comic timing + deliberate camera work + attractive environments + coherent color/shading + polished motion + purposeful reaction details.**

Use the [active benchmark v003](benchmark-v003.md) and the shared [whole-result review](../../pipeline/styles/construction.md#evaluate-the-current-production). Establish a compact visual treatment with character and environment together: silhouettes, line/shapes, palette/value hierarchy, shading/light, depth and camera language. Samtoons should retain a recognizable identity while learning craft from the references. Realism, maximum drawing detail and constant movement are not the goal.

Preserve the strongest accepted whole-character qualities from videos 001 and 002: recognizable face/hair, coherent head and body, character-specific proportions, kit/clothing, footwear and connected joints. Apply this standard across the cast. Reusing control mechanics does not justify a generic recolored face or artwork thresholds copied onto a different body. Compare whole-body and face views, then their actual transitions. Likeness and motion must survive together.

Video 001 and the accepted parts of video 002 use a hybrid of fitted illustrated meshes, editable native rigs/vector geometry, facial components and matched drawings. They are not entirely Grease Pencil films. Choose construction for the complete intended shot; do not convert everything to one method or use a static source pose as the entire performance. Describe the actual assets honestly.

The complete video 002 cut received approval, but later user feedback and the [post-film audit](videos/002-how-arsenal-won-the-league/research/benchmark-audit-v001/report.md) identify weak/repetitive acting and facing. Its accepted construction is useful evidence; its generic choreography is not the target to propagate. ## Performance and comedy

Stage each consequential beat as expectation/attention → trigger → response → changed attitude. Use original key poses and timing, then prepare the needed views, expressions and hands. Ordinary acting needs deliberate gaze, lids/brows, mouth and body agreement; listeners should react when their response matters. A repeated mouth pulse, outward palm and torso sway do not establish thought. The [supplemental Short study](research/reference-ijvy-v001/analysis.md) shows how small facial changes and distinct body poses can communicate without constant head turns.

Give gestures a purpose: an earpiece touch, phone grip, point and button press require different orientation and contact. Review the entire sleeve-to-fingertip chain, approach and recovery. Choose a hold, snap, pause or overlapping reaction for its comic purpose. Body timing must fit the performance rather than defaulting to slow, eased motion. Reuse character construction separately from the animation of a new scene.

Apply [motion planning](../../pipeline/motion-planning.md) to support transfer, force, contacts, arcs and recovery. The accepted whole-character run/jump example is an option for suitable movement, not a required eight-second test, cast or pose-swap method for every scene. A good action test cannot certify dialogue or listening. Choose a representative sequence from the new story that exposes its actual acting, mechanics, environment and editing risks before extending the construction.

## Football environments and action

For event-based stories, verify event-specific appearance, relevant footage and important relationships without inventing exact historic positions. Map the attackers, defenders, keeper, ball and offscreen roles needed to explain the action. Representative simplification is allowed; a mechanical 22-player quota is unnecessary.

Build pitch, goal, net depth, penalty-area lines, byline, touchline, corner flags, ball and cast in one coordinate system before framing. Establish scale in a wide view and retain it across shots; a flag belongs at a corner, not beside the nearest visible goalpost. Use perspective, grounding, occlusion, contact shadows and relevant layers to create depth around drawn characters. Reuse the same physical set for the same location and its reveal/reverse angles.

Author support, wind-up, visible boot/forehead contact, ball release/trajectory, follow-through and reaction as one event. Coordinate opponents and recipients; do not animate an independent ball path and hope it meets a character. Inspect consecutive frames around actual contact and the whole action at normal speed. Intentional impossible gags need clear cause and readable staging; accidental reversals, sliding and incorrect scale do not count as exaggeration.

## Sound and presentation

Direct each line for its moment—outrage, pleading, dry sarcasm, calm authority or restrained frustration. Keep each selected character voice identity consistent while varying performance. Nationality-related accents are preferences where suitable voices exist, not a reason to force an unsupported voice. Preserve the installed voice runtime/models, selected references and cached takes. Retiming alone does not require regeneration.

Plan voice, effects, music, silence, visual causes and reactions together using the [sound guide](../../pipeline/sound-planning-method.md). Let dialogue clarify necessary context without replacing visible action. Audition actual output; meters and subtitles do not prove emotional delivery. Music is optional and, under current user preference, must be free to use for the intended publication with required attribution supplied outside the picture. Speaker-labelled captions, when selected, follow meaningful speech phrases without accidental one-word flashes.

Duration, aspect ratio and pacing belong to each video's brief. No publishing schedule or turnaround promise is set. Use the [static branding kit](branding/v001/README.md) and the current [animated identity v002](branding/animated-v002/README.md). By default, finish and approve the unbranded episode first, insert the 3-second intro at its authored `branding_intro` boundary after the cold-open hook (or at 00:00 when there is no cold open), and append the 12-second outro after the final story frame. Preserve the unbranded master. Episode-specific branding changes require review; the standard clips do not.
